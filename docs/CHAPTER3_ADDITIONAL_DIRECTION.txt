CHAPTER 3 — ADDITIONAL DIRECTION
Use this together with the main Chapter 3 implementation brief and the existing story bible.
These rules are important for Chapter 3 pacing, character acting, Wendy’s introduction, humour, and continuity from Chapter 2.

1. CHAPTER 3 — LENGTH / SCALE REQUIREMENT
Chapter 3 must NOT be a short transitional chapter.
It should feel like a complete, substantial playable chapter comparable in size and pacing to Chapter 2.
Target approximately:
8–10 scenes;
roughly 5,000–7,000 words;
approximately the same overall playtime as Chapter 2;
around 3 meaningful choices if they arise naturally.
Chapter 3 should NOT be noticeably shorter than Chapter 2.
However, it should also NOT become significantly larger than Chapter 2.
Use Chapter 2 as the practical upper scale reference.
The goal is approximately:
Chapter 3 ≈ Chapter 2 in overall size.
A small difference is fine depending on natural story needs, but do not turn Chapter 3 into an 8,000–10,000+ word oversized chapter.
Prefer approximately 5,500–6,500 words if the story naturally fits that range.
Do NOT pad the chapter with:
repetitive dialogue;
unnecessary investigation loops;
filler locations;
duplicate jokes;
meaningless choices;
long exposition;
repeated Wendy teasing with no story progression.
Use the available space for meaningful content:
Pacifica / Gideon investigation;
Wendy’s proper introduction;
Pacifica ↔ Wendy conflict;
Wendy ↔ Gideon banter;
gradual suspicion that Wendy knows more than she admits;
supernatural escalation;
character reactions and animation;
discovery that Wendy took a strange object from the forest;
a strong Chapter 4 hook.
Every scene should accomplish at least one meaningful purpose:
move the mystery forward;
develop a character relationship;
reveal useful information;
increase tension;
create a meaningful consequence.
Chapter 3 should feel complete when finished while clearly leaving the Wendy/object storyline open for Chapter 4.

2. GENERAL CHARACTER STAGING / EMOTION UPDATE
Chapter 3 should use noticeably richer character expression and body-language variation than Chapters 1–2.
This does NOT mean constant bouncing, excessive camera movement, or changing sprites every line.
The goal is:
better acting through animation.
Characters should feel emotionally alive.
Prefer:
expression swaps;
subtle head/body movement;
brief flinches;
looking toward another character;
looking away during embarrassment;
slight repositioning;
restrained sprite movement;
tension poses during danger;
short reaction animations;
carefully timed pauses;
occasional foreground/background emphasis.
Avoid:
constant sprite bouncing;
animation on every sentence;
exaggerated anime reactions unless intentionally comedic;
excessive screen shake;
characters changing expressions for no reason.
Emotion should follow the dialogue and situation.

3. PACIFICA — EXPRESSIONS / ACTING
Pacifica is the more visibly expressive member of the main duo.
Use her expressions and poses naturally throughout the chapter.
She may visibly show:
genuine laughter;
small amused smiles;
embarrassment;
irritation;
surprise;
uncertainty;
suspicion;
nervousness;
controlled fear;
stronger fear during genuine danger;
relief after tension;
attempts to hide fear behind confidence.
Important:
Pacifica is brave, but she is NOT fearless.
She likes danger and adventure, but this does not mean she never gets scared.
Sometimes allow the player to see that something genuinely frightened her before she recovers her confident attitude.
Example rhythm:
sudden frightening sound;
brief frightened expression / physical flinch;
Pacifica quickly regains composure;
she claims she was not scared;
Gideon gives a deadpan reaction.
Example:
Pacifica: — Я не испугалась.
Gideon: — Я ничего не сказал.
Pacifica: — Вот и не говори.
This type of interaction fits them well.
Pacifica can also become embarrassed.
Do not make embarrassment constant or turn her into a shy character.
It should appear naturally when:
someone catches her saying something unexpectedly sincere;
she realizes she was confidently wrong;
Wendy makes an especially effective remark;
she reacts too strongly and realizes Gideon noticed;
she tries to hide concern.
Use laughter naturally.
If something genuinely makes Pacifica laugh, do not always represent it only through narration.
Where practical, combine:
expression change;
small animation;
natural voice acting;
short pause.
Pacifica should feel like a fourteen-year-old girl who is confident and adventurous but sometimes tries to look braver and more composed than she actually feels.

4. GIDEON — POKER FACE / FEAR
Gideon’s default visual language remains restrained.
His poker face is part of his personality.
Most of the time he should look:
neutral;
deadpan;
mildly skeptical;
mildly annoyed;
focused;
dryly amused.
His emotional reactions should generally be smaller than Pacifica’s.
Allow subtle reactions such as:
eyebrow movement;
side glance;
tiny smile;
brief embarrassment;
short surprised expression;
looking away when uncomfortable;
restrained concern;
slightly tense posture.
However:
Do NOT make Gideon visually emotionless in every situation.
He is calm and emotionally restrained.
He is NOT incapable of fear.
During REAL danger Gideon is allowed to visibly show fear.
Possible danger reactions:
widened eyes;
tense posture;
taking a step backward;
sudden silence;
becoming intensely focused;
faster speech;
stronger warning tone;
briefly losing his normal deadpan expression.
He should NOT become a comedy coward.
Do NOT make him:
scream constantly;
panic helplessly;
hide behind Pacifica;
freeze uselessly every time something supernatural appears.
When frightened, Gideon usually becomes more alert and analytical.
A rare strong emotional reaction should have weight precisely because he is normally restrained.
Example:
Gideon: — Пасифика.
She does not react.
Gideon’s expression changes.
Gideon: — Пасифика. Назад.
If she still ignores him:
Gideon: — ПАСИФИКА, НАЗАД.
A moment like this should feel significant because Gideon normally does not raise his voice.
The Chapter 2 climax demonstrated this well.
Continue using that principle.

5. PACIFICA + GIDEON — VISUAL CONTRAST
Their reactions should sometimes deliberately contrast.
Example:
Pacifica has a large visible reaction.
Gideon barely reacts.
Or during genuine danger:
Pacifica initially tries to act fearless.
Gideon notices something dangerous first and briefly loses his poker face.
This contrast is part of their chemistry.
Their relationship currently includes:
teasing;
early trust;
disagreements;
learning teamwork;
slowly understanding each other better.
No romance.
Their long-term direction remains close sibling-like trust.
Do not write them as if they have known each other for years.
They are still relatively new friends.

6. WENDY — CHAPTER 3 CHARACTER RULE
Chapter 3 is Wendy’s proper introduction into the main story.
Do NOT make her an immediate friend or reliable ally.
Her relationship with Pacifica and Gideon must develop gradually over multiple chapters.
At this point Wendy should primarily feel:
relaxed;
lazy;
sarcastic;
rough;
selfish;
amused by other people’s problems;
reluctant to help;
confident;
guarded;
sometimes genuinely irritating.
She is NOT evil.
She has moral limits.
But Chapter 3 should not reveal her softer side too quickly.
Do not immediately turn her into:
protective big sister;
loyal team member;
emotionally supportive friend;
someone who willingly explains everything she knows.
Those developments belong later.

7. WENDY — EXPRESSIONS / ANIMATION
Useful Wendy expressions and poses:
lazy neutral;
smug smile;
amused;
genuine short laugh;
eye roll;
irritated;
dismissive;
suspicious;
surprised;
briefly impressed;
defensive;
serious when danger becomes real.
Her posture should often look more relaxed than Pacifica’s.
She can:
lean against something;
keep her hands in pockets;
look away while talking;
casually shrug;
appear physically relaxed during arguments.
When the situation becomes genuinely dangerous, her body language should change.
A sudden transition from:
lazy / sarcastic Wendy
to:
quiet / focused Wendy
can itself tell the player:
this is actually serious.

8. WENDY ↔ GIDEON
Wendy may tease Gideon.
Possible nicknames include:
«мелкий»;
«коротышка»;
«малыш».
Do NOT use a nickname every second conversation.
The joke will become annoying if repeated constantly.
Most humour should come from interaction rather than the nickname itself.
A useful dynamic:
Wendy says something provocative.
Gideon responds with complete deadpan.
Wendy expected him to get angry or embarrassed.
He does not.
She pauses.
Then becomes more amused by him.
Example rhythm:
Wendy: — А ты вообще всегда такой серьёзный, коротышка?
Gideon: — Нет.
Pause.
Wendy: — А когда нет?
Gideon: — Когда тебя рядом нет.
Wendy pauses because she did not expect the answer.
Then laughs.
This type of interaction can slowly establish why Wendy eventually starts respecting Gideon.
Important:
Do NOT make Wendy deeply care about Gideon already.
At most Chapter 3 can establish:
amusement → curiosity → very early respect.

9. WENDY ↔ PACIFICA
Pacifica and Wendy should clash naturally.
Their current dynamic should be closer to:
mutual irritation + curiosity + reluctant attention.
Wendy may initially view Pacifica as:
spoiled;
dramatic;
controlling;
too confident.
Pacifica may view Wendy as:
selfish;
lazy;
rude;
irresponsible;
irritating.
Use this conflict for dialogue and comedy.
But do NOT make every scene an argument.
There should also be moments where one notices that the other is more competent than expected.
Example progression during Chapter 3:
irritation → conflict → surprise → one or two moments of reluctant respect.
Not friendship yet.
Do NOT resolve the Pacifica/Wendy conflict in Chapter 3.
Their stronger cooperation belongs to Chapter 4 and later chapters.

10. CHAPTER 3 — HUMOUR / BANTER UPDATE
Chapter 3 may contain slightly more humour than Chapter 2 because Wendy naturally creates more banter.
Most additional comedy should come from CHARACTER INTERACTION rather than random jokes.
Especially use:
Wendy’s dry / rough sarcasm;
Wendy casually teasing Gideon;
Wendy and Pacifica irritating each other;
Gideon’s deadpan answers to Wendy;
Pacifica reacting more visibly to Wendy’s comments;
occasional awkward pauses after someone says something unexpectedly harsh;
Wendy sometimes laughing at Pacifica or Gideon rather than with them;
Pacifica and Wendy competing verbally;
Gideon occasionally commenting on both of them.
The humour should feel spontaneous.
Do NOT make Wendy a joke machine.
Do NOT insert a punchline after every serious line.
Do NOT give every character the same style of humour.
Their comedy should reflect personality.
Pacifica: more expressive / reactive.
Gideon: deadpan / dry.
Wendy: rough / lazy / provocative.
This difference should create the comedy.

11. COMEDY SHOULD STOP WHEN DANGER BECOMES REAL
Chapter 3 can begin lighter than Chapter 2.
This is useful after the darker second half of Chapter 2.
However:
when the supernatural situation becomes genuinely dangerous, reduce the jokes naturally.
A moment where Wendy suddenly stops joking should itself be meaningful.
The player should be able to feel:
If Wendy stopped joking, something is wrong.
The same applies to Gideon.
If Gideon’s poker face breaks, the threat should feel more serious.
The target tone for Chapter 3 can therefore move approximately like:
lighter investigation / banter
→
strange mystery
→
Wendy is hiding something
→
supernatural escalation
→
real danger
→
Chapter 4 hook
Do NOT turn Chapter 3 into a pure comedy episode.
Adventure, investigation, supernatural mystery and character development remain the main story.

12. CHAPTER 2 SYMBOL — CONTINUITY
The unknown symbol discovered at the end of Chapter 2 remains an unresolved long-term mystery.
Chapter 3 SHOULD acknowledge it briefly so the Chapter 2 ending does not feel forgotten.
However:
DO NOT make the Chapter 2 symbol the main mystery of Chapter 3.
DO NOT establish that:
Wendy knows what it means;
Wendy’s object is connected to it;
the Chapter 3 supernatural threat is connected to it;
Will created it;
Mabel created it;
Dipper created it;
the Gleefuls created it;
the Journal author created it;
it belongs to a specific supernatural species;
it represents a known organization;
it is part of a larger conspiracy.
None of those connections are currently approved canon.
Its meaning remains unknown.
Suggested use:
Near the beginning of Chapter 3, Pacifica and Gideon briefly review the symbol or a sketch/photo of it.
Pacifica checks Journal #3 again.
Still nothing.
They acknowledge:
they currently do not have enough information.
Then they deliberately put the mystery aside and continue with the immediate Chapter 3 investigation.
Keep this short.
Approximately one brief conversation is enough.
The symbol should remain in the player’s memory without hijacking the Wendy storyline.
Possible rhythm:
Pacifica: — Я ещё раз проверила Дневник.
Gideon: — И?
Pacifica: — Ничего.
Gideon: — Отлично.
Pacifica: — Что именно здесь отличного?
Gideon: — Теперь мы хотя бы точно знаем, что ничего не знаем.
Pacifica: — Очень полезно.
Then move on.
Do NOT force this exact dialogue if something better fits naturally.

13. IMPORTANT — DO NOT CONNECT EVERY MYSTERY
Avoid making Gravity Falls feel artificially small.
Not every supernatural event must immediately connect to:
Will;
the Gleefuls;
the Journals;
the symbol;
the final villain.
Some mysteries can exist independently.
The Chapter 3 Wendy/object mystery should currently be allowed to stand on its own.
If a future connection is desired, it can be established later after explicit user approval.
Do not automatically create a giant conspiracy in Chapter 3.

14. CHAPTER 3 CORE STORY PRIORITY
The main Chapter 3 story remains:
brief continuity from Chapter 2;
a new strange incident near the forest / lumber area;
Pacifica and Gideon investigate;
proper introduction of Reverse Falls Wendy;
Wendy initially refuses or avoids helping;
Wendy teases Gideon and clashes with Pacifica;
it gradually becomes obvious Wendy knows more than she admits;
investigation continues;
supernatural problem escalates;
Pacifica and Gideon discover Wendy took a strange object from the forest;
that object is involved in the current problem;
Wendy still does not become a full ally;
Chapter 3 ends with a strong continuation hook into Chapter 4.
Do NOT completely resolve the Wendy/object storyline in Chapter 3.
Chapter 4 continues this mini-arc.

15. VERY IMPORTANT — WENDY’S ALLIANCE MUST BE GRADUAL
Wendy becoming an ally is a multi-chapter progression.
Do not rush it.
Chapter 3:
conflict / amusement / reluctance / first small respect
Chapter 4:
forced cooperation / stronger conflict / beginning of trust
Later chapters:
respect / friendship / eventual protective instincts
Do not skip directly to the later stages.
The player should be able to feel the relationship changing slowly over time.

16. FINAL QUALITY TARGET
Chapter 3 should feel like:
approximately Chapter 2’s scale;
slightly lighter at first;
more character-driven comedy because Wendy is present;
richer visual acting;
Pacifica visibly expressive;
Gideon mostly poker-faced;
Gideon’s fear visible only when danger genuinely warrants it;
Wendy sarcastic, rough and not yet friendly;
Pacifica/Wendy conflict alive and entertaining;
Wendy/Gideon banter beginning naturally;
an actual supernatural investigation;
a real escalation of danger;
a meaningful reveal involving Wendy’s forest object;
a strong cliffhanger into Chapter 4.
Do not sacrifice mystery or pacing just to add more dialogue.
Do not make the chapter shorter than Chapter 2 simply because its outline looks simpler.
Do not make it significantly longer either.
The final result should feel like a full proper episode of the game, not setup for the next one.
